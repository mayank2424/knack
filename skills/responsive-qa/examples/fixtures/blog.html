Why cache at the edge

Most of our traffic is anonymous and read-heavy, so every millisecond spent going back to the origin is wasted. Moving rendering closer to readers cut median latency in half. The full benchmark lives at https://github.com/devlog-example/edge-cache-benchmarks/blob/main/results/2026-09-full-run.md.

Before the change, a cold request from Sydney took 840ms. After it, the same request took 190ms. Warm requests dropped from 320ms to under 40ms.

Cache keys that actually work

The trick is to keep the cache key small. We strip tracking parameters and normalise the Accept-Language header before hashing:

const key = new URL(request.url); key.searchParams.forEach((_, k) => k.startsWith('utm_') && key.searchParams.delete(k)); return caches.default.match(key.toString());

Each region keeps its own copy, and we purge by surrogate key when an article changes.

Results

Origin load fell by 81%, and our hosting bill followed. The next step is caching personalised fragments with a short TTL.

We also learned that the hardest part is invalidation, not caching. Tag every response with the entities it depends on and purge by tag.
